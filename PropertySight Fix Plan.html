Remediation Plan · Companion to the New-Customer Review

Fixing the funnel: a sequenced plan to turn a strong product into a strong conversion.

Every issue from the review, grouped into three phases by impact and effort. The first phase is two weeks of cheap, mechanical fixes that repair the worst leaks. Each task carries scope, the concrete changes, and a testable "done" bar.

Phase 1 — 2 weeks
Stop the leaks
Pricing live on the site; sell the free-scan value; message the 3-year retention as a promise.
Phase 2 — weeks 3–5
Build trust
Social proof, demo video, Terms/Refund/FAQ, real support, promote the guarantee.
Phase 3 — weeks 6–9
Reduce friction
Simplify the currency model, email sign-in, brand-name cleanup, drop ad-for-credits.

Scope tags: SITE = marketing site · APP = Flutter client · BACKEND = server/billing. Effort is rough dev-time, not calendar.

Phase 1 · Critical

Stop the leaks

Target: repair site→install and first-scan→value

Three changes, none a rebuild. They fix the two worst joints in the funnel and set expectations honestly. Pricing (F2) is already implemented on the site — the rest follow.

F3 Lead with what a single scan already gives — for free SiteApp~2–3 days
CorrectionEarlier framing that the scan is "empty until you pay" was wrong. The base scan already returns a genuinely useful record at no credit cost — that's the hook the funnel should sell, not hide.
Free scan delivers
  • Owner of record (name on title)
  • Verified address & coordinates
  • Property facts — year built, beds, baths, square footage
  • Last sale price + date
  • Tax / assessor market value
  • Four record-based lead signals — Vacant, Long-term owner, Absentee, Investor-owned

F4 Message the 3-year data-retention promise clearly AppSite~1–2 days
CorrectionThe review overstated this. Finds are not deleted at cancellation. Per the sunset policy, an ex-subscriber's data is retained for a full 36 months (3 years) from the date the subscription ends, and re-subscribing at any point fully resets the clock. Trial-only accounts are held 6 months.
Real issueThe problem is tone, not policy: the in-app banner counts down "your finds will be deleted in N months" for the last 6 months, which reads as a threat rather than the generous 3-year window it actually is.
Changes
  • Reframe the retention copy positively: "We keep your finds for 3 years after your plan ends — resubscribe anytime to pick up where you left off."
  • Soften the final-window banner from a deletion countdown to a re-activation nudge.
  • State the 3-year retention as a trust feature on the site and in the FAQ, and offer a CSV export as an extra safety net.
DoneRetention reads as a promise ("3 years, always yours to reactivate"), not a deletion threat.
Phase 2 · High

Build trust

Target: raise the trust ceiling from "unproven"
T1 Add social proof & a 30-second demo video Site~2–4 days
Changes
  • 3–5 real agent testimonials (name, market, headshot) placed near the hero and the pricing block.
  • A short screen-capture video of a live scan resolving to a real record — the moment the static mockups can't sell.
  • Store-rating badge once listings are live; brokerage/association logos if any.
DoneHero and pricing each carry at least one form of third-party validation; a demo video is above the fold on mobile.
T2 Publish Terms, Refund policy, FAQ & real support Site~2–3 days
Changes
  • Add Terms of Service and a Refund policy page (only Privacy is linked today).
  • FAQ answering the pessimistic defaults: Is this legal? Where's the data from? What's a scan vs a credit? What happens if I cancel? Is contact data DNC-safe?
  • A real support path — help email with a stated response time, ideally a help center.
DoneFooter links to Terms, Refund, FAQ, and Support; every top objection has a written answer.
T3 Promote the refund-on-no-data guarantee Site~2 hr
WhyRefund-on-unverified is real and enforced in code — the product's best trust asset — but it's buried in one sentence.
Changes
  • Make "You're only charged for data we actually find" a headline near pricing, not a footnote.
  • Pair it with the DNC-flagging and named-sources story from the Sources page; surface Sources more prominently in the nav.
DoneThe guarantee appears as a first-class selling point adjacent to the price.
Phase 3 · Medium

Reduce friction

Target: ease comprehension & access at the buy moment
R1 Simplify — or clearly explain — the scan/credit model AppSiteLow→High
WhyTwo currencies + conversions + ads is a heavy comprehension tax sitting right on the purchase decision.
Changes
  • Best: collapse to a single currency so a user reasons about one balance.
  • If not: a one-screen "scans vs credits" explainer on site + in-app, and a running cost-per-lead readout so spend is legible.
  • Remove the "convert 1 credit to 5 scans" dialog from the critical path; make conversions a settings-level action, not a checkout decision.
DoneA first-time buyer can state, in one sentence, what a scan costs and what a credit buys.
R2 Drop "watch ads to earn credits" App~1 day
WhyAn ad-for-credits path on a professional lead tool cheapens the premium framing and, by association, the perceived data quality.
ChangesRemove the ad-reward flow from the out-of-credits and top-up surfaces; if a free-credit mechanic is wanted, replace it with a referral credit (which also drives growth).
DoneNo ad-watching prompt anywhere in the paid experience.
R3 Add email sign-in & lock the brand name App~2–3 days
Changes
  • Add email/password or magic-link sign-in alongside Google/Apple for agents who won't link a personal identity — keep it anchored by the anti-refarm marker.
  • Audit production so only "PropertySight / Point · Scan · Discover" ever appears; retire the internal "Seepopi / Point. Identify. Explore." defaults.
  • Give the generic "Couldn't sign in" error an actionable next step (retry / contact support).
DoneA user can create an account without a Google/Apple ID; no build ever shows the old brand name.

How we'll know it worked

Instrument the funnel before Phase 1 so the fixes are measurable, then watch these joints:

  • Site → install (F2, F3): pricing-page views and the free-scan value story — does clear pricing plus a visible "what one scan gives" lift install intent?
  • First scan → value felt (F3): share of new users who complete a scan and see the free record, and trial→paid conversion.
  • Retention (F4, R1, R2): voluntary (non-coerced) renewal rate, cancellation reasons, and referral rate — does positive retention messaging reduce churn anxiety?
  • Trust (T1–T3): pricing-page → install rate and time-on-site after proof and price are added.
